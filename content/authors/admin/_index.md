I am a Research Fellow at the CAUSALab and Zhu Family Center for Global Cancer Prevention at the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health. I also serve as a Big Data Health Scientist at the Veteran’s Health Administration, the nation’s largest integrated healthcare system.
{style="text-align: justify;"}
My work combines advanced causal inference methods with high-dimensional healthcare data to inform comparative effectiveness decisions and advance epidemiologic methods.
{style="text-align: justify;"}
Specifically, my research aims to (i) provide evidence to support personalized pharmacological and behavioral cancer control strategies, (ii) combine innovative causal and machine learning methods with multimodal datasets, and (iii) identify methodological challenges that arise in these areas along with appropriate analytic solutions to address them.
{style="text-align: justify;"}
Throughout my work, I focus on estimating the effects of dynamic strategies, that is, personalized strategies which are tailored to an individual’s evolving characteristics over time. While these personalized strategies are often of prime interest for clinical decision-making and precision medicine, valid estimation of their effects requires g-methods. I combine g-methods with high-dimensional and multimodal data sources, including electronic health records, healthcare claims, cancer registries, prospective cohorts, molecular biomarkers, and medical images. I also have expertise in the design, analysis, and interpretation of target trial emulation studies in oncology and other clinical areas. In addition, my work prioritizes the inclusion of individuals who have been underrepresented in randomized trials of cancer interventions, such as older adults.
{style="text-align: justify;"}
I am also passionate about teaching advanced epidemiologic and biostatistical methods and mentoring early-career researchers. 
{style="text-align: justify;"}
